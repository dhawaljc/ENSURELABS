See it to build it.

The Research

The best way to learn something is to do it.

Understanding how a building performs — or how to construct one safely — is hard to teach from a textbook or a lecture. We put people inside virtual buildings, virtual construction sites, and virtual extreme environments instead.

Using eye-tracking, EEG, and behavioral data, we study how people learn and perform in unfamiliar, complex, and high-stakes environments. What spatial cues do people rely on? How does visual information overload affect task performance? How can training environments be designed to accelerate skill acquisition and retention?

This work is funded by NASA for astronaut training research, by NSF for construction workforce development, and by the New Frontiers in Research Fund for climate education and community resilience. The technology is new. The insight is old: doing beats observing.

What We Found
When you float in space,
your brain gets very confused.

We put people in VR headsets that simulate floating in zero gravity. We measured their brains with EEG sensors and tracked their eyes. Then we trained them — and watched the confusion shrink.

🌀
Without VR training — first time in zero gravity
Your brain is used to "down" being down. In space or on a tall building, that signal disappears. Suddenly you can't tell which way is up, where things are, or how far away they seem.

After VR training — brain has practiced

Our Platforms
Environmental Education

BIMThermoVR

BIMThermoVR puts high school students inside a virtual single-family house. They adjust design variables — window-to-wall ratios, insulation levels, orientation — and watch how those choices affect embodied and operational energy outcomes in real time. Pre- and post-instruction surveys measure changes in environmental awareness and conceptual understanding of building sustainability. The goal: make energy and carbon tangible for young people before they become the designers, engineers, and policymakers of tomorrow.

New Frontiers in Research Fund · NFRF
Construction Workforce Training

Virtual Construction Lab

The Virtual Construction Lab is a gamified VR interface for training workers in construction assembly. Users progress through levels of development — from simplified Lego-style wall builds to detailed reinforced concrete assemblies with support tools. We evaluate user experience, behavior, and learning progression within the immersive environment, studying whether virtual training can substitute for resource-intensive physical field labs without sacrificing skill transfer.

NSF · New Frontiers in Research Fund
Spatial Cognition in Extreme Environments

Astronaut Training & Spatial Cognition Research

How do people navigate environments that offer few spatial cues? What happens to spatial memory and task performance under the cognitive load of a suit, a mission, and an alien environment? Working with NASA-funded research, we use eye-tracking, EEG, and immersive simulation to study spatial cognition in altered and extreme conditions — research with implications for emergency evacuation, disaster response, and human performance in any environment that strips away familiar cues.
