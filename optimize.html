Iterate to optimize

The best design is discovered, not assumed.

Material choices, energy efficiency, durability, and construction strategies are inherently interconnected. Improving one objective often affects another. We design and integrate forward and inverse multi-objective optimization (MOO) frameworks that identify design solutions balancing embodied and operational impacts while satisfying project goals, site conditions, regulatory requirements, and practical design constraints.

Energy, carbon and water, under future climates

Impacts are dynamic and multi-faceted

Tomorrow’s buildings cannot be designed using yesterday’s assumptions.

Buildings constructed today will operate under future climates, evolving electricity grids, emerging construction technologies, and changing resource availability. ENSURE Lab advances dynamic life cycle assessment frameworks that integrate future climate scenarios, energy transitions, and technology evolution while simultaneously evaluating energy, carbon emissions, and water use through an integrated energy–carbon–water nexus perspective.

Projects

Nexus-centered prognostic multi-objective optimization

Buildings impose environmental burdens through both operational energy (OE) during use phase and embodied flows embedded in materials and processes (e.g., manufacturing, construction, transport, maintenance, repair/replacement, and end-of-life). These flows draw on primary and secondary energy and freshwater directly (site activities) and indirectly (material and energy supply chains). A carbon-centered optimization process can therefore shift burdens and unintentionally increase freshwater consumption. In view of accelerating freshwater scarcity, optimization must address the energy-carbon-water (ECW) nexus, not just carbon in isolation.

In this research we propose a prognostic, rather than diagnostic, optimization framework that explicitly anticipates non-stationary boundary conditions (climate and energy systems). The framework jointly optimizes embodied energy (EE), operational energy, life-cycle carbon, and embodied water subject to performance and feasibility constraints. We employ metaheuristics (e.g., NSGA-II) on envelope decision variables (e.g., insulation, glazing, window-to-wall ratio, orientation, assembly configurations), evaluating candidates and selecting the optimal solution with current and projected weather (morphed from climate scenarios), and with evolving energy-economy parameters (end-use intensities, electricity generation mix, and usage patterns). This prognostic stance examines design robustness to future climate and grid trajectories.

The research program conducts multi-tier optimization of real-world commercial buildings across all United States sub-climate zones to: (1) quantify EE-OE interdependencies by measuring an EE factor that represents the EE expense of saving one unit of OE; (2) re-evaluate energy-focused design decisions from an ECW nexus lens; and (3) investigate how fast-changing energy mix and use and climate variability may influence future EE-OE interdependencies. Education and dissemination objectives are aligned with the research aims. We integrate physics-based energy modeling and virtual-reality (VR)-enabled learning into curricula and research training to: (a) establish a diagnostic-to-prognostic pedagogy at the university level, (b) strengthen environmental sustainability education in high schools, and (c) translate tools and guidance to architects, engineers, and home builders, enabling broader, societally relevant adoption of ECW-consistent design practice.
