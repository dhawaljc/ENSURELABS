Buildings, measured across lifetimes.

ENSURE Lab studies buildings across lifetimes, generating knowledge to reduce carbon emissions and strengthen climate resilience.

We are an interdisciplinary team of researchers at Texas A&M University's College of Architecture studying how buildings, materials, and communities perform under changing environmental conditions. Our work combines life cycle assessment, climate-aware modeling, data-driven decision support, materials research, community engagement, and immersive training tools, creating the scientific foundation for a lower-carbon, more resilient built environment.

Every brick spends energy
Life Cycle Assessment & Environmental Impacts

Matter matters.

Every material in a building carries a hidden carbon cost: in the mines, the factories, the trucks, and the landfills. Most of the world counts only the energy a building uses while occupied. We count everything. Across the full life cycle, from raw extraction to end-of-life demolition, we measure what buildings are actually made of and what that costs the planet. Because you can't reduce what you don't measure.

Climate-Aware Design & Optimization

Buildings outlive their blueprints.

A building designed for today's climate will live through tomorrow's. By 2080, the city it stands in may be hotter, drier, and more volatile than anything its engineers imagined. We optimize buildings not for the world they're born into, but for the world they'll age through, jointly minimizing energy, carbon, and water across every future climate scenario we can model.

Sustainability-Resilience Nexus & Living Labs

Resilience is personal.

Port Arthur, Texas has flooded repeatedly. Medellín has faced decades of instability. Indramayu watches the sea rise. Climate resilience isn't an abstract policy goal for these communities: it's survival. We don't design solutions for them. We design with them. Our Living Lab approach puts affected residents, local businesses, and community organizations at the center of every decision, ensuring that what we build together is something they can actually use.

Holding the building’s future
Human-Centered Immersive Training & Simulation

See it to build it.

Understanding how a building performs, or how to construct one safely, is hard to teach from a textbook. We put people inside virtual buildings, virtual construction sites, and virtual extreme environments instead. Students adjust insulation and window ratios and watch energy use change in real time. Workers assemble walls block by block in a simulation before they ever touch a real one. Astronauts train for environments they've never been in. The technology is new. The idea is ancient: the best way to learn something is to do it.
