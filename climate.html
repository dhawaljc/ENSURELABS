Climate-Aware Design & Optimization

Buildings outlive
their blueprints.

The Research

We optimize for the world buildings will age through.

A building constructed today will stand for 50, 80, perhaps 100 years. But its insulation levels, window ratios, and mechanical systems were almost certainly designed for the climate of the day it was built — not the climate it will actually inhabit.

We study how design choices perform under changing climate and energy systems. Using metaheuristic optimization on envelope decision variables with morphed future weather data and evolving grid projections, we jointly optimize embodied energy, operational energy, life-cycle carbon, and embodied water — the energy–carbon–water nexus — across all U.S. sub-climate zones.

This prognostic stance — anticipating rather than reacting to climate change — is what separates our work from conventional energy modeling. Funded by the National Science Foundation.

What We Found
A building built today will still be standing

The Climate Challenge
2030
Near-term projection
Modest warming. Buildings designed today may still perform near specification. Energy grid decarbonization begins to shift embodied carbon calculus.
2050
Mid-century
Significant heat stress. Cooling loads diverge substantially from design assumptions. Envelope thermal performance degrades faster under prolonged heat exposure.
2075
Late-century
Extreme events become more frequent. Buildings near coasts, flood plains, and wildfire zones face compound risks their designs never anticipated.
2100
End-of-century
A building designed today is still standing. The climate it was built for no longer exists. Our research ensures the gap between design and reality is as small as possible.

We integrate CMIP6 projections and CORDEX downscaled climate data, alongside evolving grid mix and energy use intensities, to ensure that the buildings we optimize today remain resilient across the full range of futures they might inhabit.

Research Projects
01
Nexus-Centered Prognostic Multi-Objective Optimization
We jointly optimize embodied energy, operational energy, life-cycle carbon, and embodied water using NSGA-II metaheuristics on envelope decision variables — evaluated against current and projected future weather and energy grid conditions across all U.S. sub-climate zones.
NSF-Funded
02
Forward & Inverse Predictive Analytics
Forward neural network emulators predict building performance in milliseconds. Inverse goal-conditioned models flip the workflow: specify target carbon or comfort levels, receive feasible design configurations back — with full explainability of the driving trade-offs.
NSF · TAMU RLF
03
Hygrothermal Climate Stress & Envelope Performance
In collaboration with Penn State, we examine how climate-induced temperature and humidity swings degrade building envelopes over 100+ year life cycles — translating accelerated weathering test data into dynamic LCA estimates under CMIP6 scenarios.
NSF · Penn State Collaboration
