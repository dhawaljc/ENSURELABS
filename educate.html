Experiencing the invisible

People care about what they see and understand. We turn invisible environmental processes into experiences students, professionals and communities can walk through.

Projects

Serious Games for Environmental Awareness and Construction Training

BIMThermoVR. Our team is developing a gamified, immersive Virtual Reality (VR) platform to improve high school students’ understanding of the building sector’s energy and environmental impacts. Within a virtual single-family house, learners navigate the environment and adjust design parameters such as, window-to-wall ratio, orientation, and insulation levels to observe how these changes affect embodied and operational energy outcomes. We employ BIMThermoVR to deliver engaging, interactive activities and evaluate changes in environmental awareness using pre- and post-instruction surveys.

Virtual Construction Lab (VCL). VCL is a gamified VR interface that provides experiential training in the virtual construction of wall sections using digital materials and support systems. The primary objective is to simulate construction workflows across Levels of Development (LOD): users begin with a simplified “Lego-block” wall, progress to a more detailed assembly with concrete blocks and reinforcement, and ultimately employ support tools (e.g., a virtual ladder) to complete a detailed wall build.

E³ — Enrichment Experiences in Engineering

A three-week summer research placement that puts a K–12 teacher inside the lab. The teacher works alongside the faculty sponsor and doctoral students in Francis Hall, then carries the material back into a school curriculum. The programme is built around three research tasks, each ending in work the teacher designs for their own classroom.

Task 1 — How buildings consume energy over their life cycle, and how that becomes carbon. Life cycle thinking and case studies on construction materials and buildings: design strategies, net-zero energy and carbon-negative concepts, life cycle assessment, and the nexus approach. The teacher learns the life cycle energy model of a typical residential building, what a building envelope does, and where embodied energy arises across initial construction, maintenance and replacement, and demolition.

Task 2 — Why operational and embodied impacts conflict. Heating, cooling and lighting loads are interdependent with the embodied impacts of material choices, which makes environmentally conscious decisions harder than they look. The teacher simulates a simple residential building, applies design measures, and watches energy use and material quantity move in opposite directions — the reason energy optimization is a multi-objective problem needing computational tools such as genetic algorithms.

Task 3 — Water, VR, and future uncertainty. Energy-based design decisions can conflict with water use, so the teacher works through the water implications of renewable and non-renewable sources. They then use the lab’s VR tools and build lesson plans around them, closing with how weather- and energy-economy uncertainties shape life cycle modelling from an energy–carbon–water nexus standpoint.
