Life Cycle Assessment & Environmental Impacts

Matter matters.

The Research

Every building carries a hidden carbon cost.

Most of the world counts only the energy a building uses while occupied. We count everything. The steel, the concrete, the insulation, the glass — each material carries an embodied carbon cost that begins long before a building opens its doors and continues long after it closes them.

ENSURE Lab's life cycle assessment research quantifies embodied and operational impacts across materials, building systems, and service lives of 50–100 years. We developed hybrid input–output models that integrate environmentally extended economic data with building-specific bills of quantities — producing scenario-robust life-cycle inventories consistent with A1–A5, B-stage, and C-stage system boundaries.

Our methods and datasets are used in federally funded studies, university research programs, and industry applications across the AEC sector. The goal: make life-cycle environmental performance a primary design criterion, not an afterthought.

Research Projects
01
Life Cycle Energy & Environmental Assessment (LCEEA)
We develop macroeconomic input–output hybrid models to estimate embodied energy and carbon of construction materials and services across multi-tier supply chains. Our BIM-integrated tools bring these estimates directly into early design workflows.
NSF · TAMU · TAMU ASCEND
02
LCA of Bio-Based Materials for 3D Printing
We conducted cradle-to-grave LCA of hempcrete for large-scale extrusion-based 3D printing — integrating process-based and input–output methodologies — and assessed clay composites for circularity and mechanical performance per unit of environmental impact.
DOE ARPA-E · TAMU X-Grant
03
Nexus-Centered Prognostic Multi-Objective Optimization
We propose a prognostic optimization framework that jointly minimizes embodied energy, operational energy, life-cycle carbon, and embodied water — treating the energy–carbon–water nexus as a whole rather than optimizing for a single metric and shifting burdens elsewhere.
NSF-Funded
