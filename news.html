Maryam Abbasi Kamazani received the Best Presentation Award at the 3rd International Net-Zero Future Conference in Darmstadt, Germany, for “A Methodological Framework for Surrogate-Based Operational Energy Prediction Using Envelope Material Variables.” The research develops a surrogate-modeling framework that uses envelope material variables to predict operational energy. ENSURE Lab Presents five Research Papers on Net-Zero Carbon and Climate-Resilient Buildings in Germany

Manish Dixit presented three research papers highlighting the work of ENSURE Lab researchers Mana Nemati Aghdam, Sejal Sanjay Shanbhag, and Yasaman Norouzi at the Net-Zero Future 2026 Conference in Darmstadt, Germany.

Read more

Using Port Arthur, Texas, as a case study, Mana’s research introduces an integrated framework for evaluating building envelope materials based on life cycle environmental impacts and resistance to climate hazards, including wind, moisture, fire, and thermal stresses. The study advances ENSURE Lab’s vision of integrating climate mitigation and adaptation into building design.

In collaboration with Penn State researchers Julian Wang, Mohammad Elmi, Hannah Yang, and Jessica Nicole Miklos, Sejal’s research investigates how extreme hygrothermal fluctuations can accelerate building envelope deterioration, potentially increasing both embodied and operational carbon emissions over a building’s life cycle.

Yasaman’s research explores the optimization of embodied and operational global warming potential (GWP) in residential buildings constructed using 3D-printable hempcrete. Spanning all 16 U.S. climate subzones, the study examines the challenges and opportunities of achieving net-zero life cycle GWP across diverse climatic conditions.

Another presentation introduced a surrogate-based operational energy prediction framework integrating physics-based building energy simulations with machine learning. The research aims to enable computationally efficient energy predictions and support informed building envelope design and optimization, advancing the application of artificial intelligence in sustainable building design.

An international collaborative study involving researchers from Canada, the United States, and Norway presented a place-based, climate-adaptive key performance indicator (KPI) framework for the Indonesia Living Lab. The framework extends conventional net-zero assessments by incorporating flood resilience, service continuity, repairability, and maintenance needs, highlighting the importance of aligning environmental sustainability metrics with local climate vulnerabilities and community priorities.

Together, these studies demonstrate ENSURE Lab’s commitment to interdisciplinary and international collaboration, advancing integrated approaches to environmental assessment, AI-driven optimization, material innovation, and climate resilience for sustainable and adaptive built environments.

ENSURE Lab Welcomes Two New Doctoral Researchers Advancing Climate-Responsive and Low-Carbon Building Science

ENSURE Lab is delighted to welcome Shilpi Dua and Soraya Mokhtari, who joined the lab in 2026 as doctoral researchers. Bringing complementary expertise in architecture, sustainable design, urban planning, and informatics, they will advance innovative research on building-envelope performance, sustainable materials, and climate-responsive construction.

Read more

Shilpi Dua brings 15 years of professional experience in architecture, design, and entrepreneurship, with a strong focus on sustainable construction. Her doctoral research explores multifunctional, bio-based, and nature-inspired building-envelope materials capable of adapting to changing thermal conditions. By investigating dynamic material behavior and climate-responsive design strategies, her work aims to reduce building heating and cooling demands, particularly under increasingly warmer and more variable climatic conditions.

Soraya Mokhtari brings an interdisciplinary background in architecture, urban design, and informatics. Her doctoral research investigates how extreme temperature and moisture fluctuations, under current and projected future climates, influence the deterioration, durability, and life cycle environmental impacts of building-envelope materials and assemblies. By integrating hygrothermal performance analysis, climate projections, life cycle assessment, and machine learning, her work seeks to inform more durable, resilient, and lower-carbon building design.

Together, their research strengthens ENSURE Lab's mission to advance science-informed, environmentally sustainable, and climate-resilient built environments through interdisciplinary innovation, advanced modeling, and the development of next-generation building materials and systems.

We warmly welcome Shilpi and Soraya to ENSURE Lab and look forward to their contributions to advancing sustainable and resilient construction research.

ENSURE Lab Completes NSF Regional I-Corps Project on AI-Powered Environmental Product Declaration Generation

ENSURE Lab successfully completed an NSF Regional I-Corps project exploring the commercialization potential of artificial intelligence (AI)-powered Environmental Product Declaration (EPD) generation and data development. ENSURE Lab researchers Sejal Sanjay Shanbhag, Maryam Abbasi Kamazani, and Yasaman Norouzi conducted extensive market discovery through 31 interviews with sustainability and life cycle assessment (LCA) consultants, EPD developers and verifiers, and architecture, engineering, and construction professionals.

Read more

The project investigated industry needs, commercialization opportunities, and barriers to adopting AI-generated EPDs, with particular emphasis on data reliability, transparency, verification, and professional trust. The findings provide valuable insights into the potential for AI-driven solutions to streamline EPD development and improve access to reliable environmental data for informed, low-carbon building design and construction.

ENSURE Lab Showcases Innovative Net-Zero and Climate-Resilient Building Research in Cape Town, South Africa

Manish Dixit and Sejal Shanbhag presented ENSURE Lab research at the 2025 Net-Zero Future Conference, hosted by the University of Cape Town, South Africa. The presentations showcased innovative research by doctoral researchers Mana Nemati Aghdam, Yasaman Norouzi, and Maryam Abbasi Kamazani, advancing integrated approaches to sustainable and climate-resilient building design.

Read more

Mana Nemati Aghdam’s research introduced an integrated rating framework that combines life cycle environmental sustainability indicators with location-specific climate resilience metrics. The framework seeks to bridge the gap between climate mitigation and adaptation, enabling more informed decisions about building materials and systems that minimize environmental impacts while improving resistance to climate hazards.

Yasaman Norouzi’s research presented an AI-driven computational framework for optimizing the geometric configurations of 3D-printed hempcrete walls. By integrating advanced optimization methods with thermal performance assessment, the study explores opportunities to minimize global warming potential while enhancing the thermal efficiency of innovative bio-based building systems.

Maryam Abbasi Kamazani’s research advanced a holistic, performance-driven framework for optimizing interconnected energy, carbon, and water impacts in building design. Through multi-objective optimization, the study examines environmental trade-offs and synergies to support more balanced, resource-efficient, and environmentally responsible design decisions.

Together, these studies highlight ENSURE Lab’s commitment to advancing AI-integrated environmental assessment, regenerative construction materials, and climate-resilient design, while fostering international scientific collaboration toward a sustainable and net-zero built environment.
