Sustainability–Resilience Nexus & Living Labs

Resilience is personal.

The Research

We design with communities, not for them.

Port Arthur, Texas has flooded repeatedly. Medellín has faced decades of instability. Indramayu watches the sea rise. Climate resilience isn't an abstract policy goal for these communities — it is survival.

In our two-pronged approach, we first connect life cycle carbon emissions with material durability and service life — helping designers understand how long materials last and how that changes their environmental footprint. Then we take those findings into the field, co-designing adaptation strategies with affected residents, local businesses, and community organizations.

This is a multinational collaboration across seven countries — United States, Canada, Norway, United Kingdom, Switzerland, Indonesia, and Colombia — funded by Canada's New Frontiers in Research Fund.

Three Living Labs

United States
Port Arthur, Texas
Located on the Texas Gulf Coast, Port Arthur has experienced repeated flooding, petrochemical exposure, and economic disinvestment. Our primary Living Lab engages residents, local nonprofits, and city officials in co-designing resilient building strategies for a community that has seen too little attention from the research community.
Focus: Flood resilience · Built environment adaptation · Community preparedness
Colombia
Medellín, Colombia
Once one of the world's most dangerous cities, Medellín has undergone a remarkable urban transformation. Our Living Lab here focuses on how informal settlements in hillside neighborhoods can be made more resilient to the increasing intensity of rainfall events and landslide risk driven by climate change.
Focus: Hillside housing · Landslide risk · Urban transformation
Indonesia
Indramayu, Indonesia
Indramayu faces sea-level rise, land subsidence, and threats to agriculture and fisheries that have sustained its communities for generations. Our research here extends beyond buildings into the broader resilience of food systems, water management, and livelihoods under accelerating climate pressure.
Focus: Coastal resilience · Agriculture · Watershed management
Our Framework

A two-pronged approach to sustainable resilience

01
Carbon meets durability
We connect environmental sustainability — measured through life cycle carbon emissions — to climate resilience through durability and service life assessments. A material that degrades faster requires more replacement, generating more carbon. A more durable material costs more upfront but less over time. Our analytical framework reveals these trade-offs at the design stage, enabling material selection decisions grounded in both carbon intensity and resilience performance.
02
Co-design with communities
We apply a Living Lab approach to ensure communities are involved in both identifying key climate challenges and designing solutions. Our participatory co-design model engages affected residents, local businesses, and organizations active in disaster preparedness, response, and recovery. The goal: solutions that are socially acceptable, technically sound, and actually implementable within the cost and supply chain constraints of the communities they serve.
