Every second · 1,186 tonnes

A ball 105 metres wide

CO₂ is invisible, so give it a volume. At sea level pressure one tonne of it fills about 506 cubic metres: a ball ten metres across. One second of the world's fossil emissions is a ball 105 metres wide, taller than the Statue of Liberty with her pedestal and torch.

Every minute · 71,157 tonnes

Clearing the Eiffel Tower

Sixty seconds makes a sphere 410 metres across, comfortably over the Eiffel Tower at 330 metres. By weight that is about 180 fully loaded Boeing 747s, every minute, without a pause.

Every hour · 4.27 million tonnes

Twice the tallest building

An hour's worth is 1.6 kilometres across, nearly twice the height of the Burj Khalifa. It weighs roughly 19,000 Statues of Liberty.

Every day · 102.5 million tonnes

Half of Everest

A single day makes a ball 4.6 kilometres across, more than half the height of Everest. In mass: about 17 Great Pyramids of Giza, or 10,000 Eiffel Towers. Then the day ends and another begins.

Every year · 37.4 billion tonnes

Three times higher than aircraft fly

A year of fossil CO₂ is a sphere 33 kilometres across. It stands 3.7 times the height of Everest, and its top sits deep in the stratosphere, three times higher than airliners cruise. By weight, roughly 75 times every human being alive.

37.4
billion tonnes · fossil CO₂ · 2024 · a new record

The 2024 Global Carbon Budget confirmed fossil CO₂ emissions rose 0.8% above the previous year's record. In the researchers' own words, there is still "no sign" the world has reached a peak.

The atmosphere
doesn't forget

CO₂ emitted today will warm the planet for hundreds of years. Atmospheric concentration reached 422.8 ppm in 2024, the highest in over 3 million years, 52% above pre-industrial levels. At current rates, the remaining 1.5°C carbon budget runs out within five years.
