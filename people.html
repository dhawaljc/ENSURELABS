Director & Principal Investigator

Manish Dixit, Ph.D., LEED AP

Professor and Clark Construction Group Endowed Professor · Department of Construction Science · Texas A&M University

Manish Dixit is the Director of the ENSURE Lab and a Professor and Clark Construction Group Endowed Professor in the Department of Construction Science at Texas A&M University. He has more than a decade of professional experience in the building design and construction industry, with a primary focus on commercial construction.

His research interests encompass life-cycle energy and environmental modeling, facilities management, virtual and augmented reality, embodied energy assessment, additive manufacturing in construction, Building Information Modeling, and spatial cognition in altered or extreme environments.

The U.S. Department of Energy endorsed his nomination as the National Expert for IEA Annex 57 and Annex 72, two prominent multinational collaborative research projects that have substantially advanced global efforts to standardize life-cycle energy modeling for buildings.

His research has been funded by the National Science Foundation, NASA, the U.S. Department of Energy, the New Frontiers in Research Fund, and Vanitec.

Current researchers

Faezeh Salehi

Ph.D. Candidate · Construction Science

Faezeh joined the Ph.D. program in Construction Science in January 2022 and recently achieved doctoral candidacy. Her research focuses on spatial cognition, virtual and augmented reality, and human factors in extreme environments such as microgravity and Mars-like conditions. Integrating eye-tracking, EEG and advanced data analytics, her work investigates how altered visuospatial conditions affect spatial ability, perception and performance. She has published in Virtual Reality, Human Factors in Virtual Environments and Game Design, and Intelligent Human Systems Integration, contributing to NASA- and NSF-funded projects on astronaut training and spatial cognition modelling.

Research focusSpatial cognition · VR/AR · Eye-tracking · EEG · NASA Astronaut Training

Shilpi Dua

Graduate Research Assistant

Shilpi is an architect with 15 years of professional experience spanning architecture, design, and entrepreneurship, with a strong emphasis on sustainable construction and environmentally responsive design. She joined ENSURE Lab in 2026, where she also serves as a Graduate Research Assistant and contributes to research at the intersection of building science, materials, and climate-responsive construction.

Her research focuses on the multidimensional thermal behavior of building-envelope materials and the development of more adaptive, climate-responsive construction solutions. She is particularly interested in nature-inspired mechanisms and bio-based materials that can enable envelope components to respond more effectively to changing ambient conditions rather than relying solely on static thermal properties. Through this work, she aims to explore new pathways for improving envelope performance under increasingly variable and warmer climate conditions. The broader goal of her research is to reduce building heating and, especially, cooling demand while supporting more energy-efficient, low-impact, and climate-responsive buildings.

Research focusEnvelope thermal behavior · Bio-based materials · Nature-inspired design · Climate-responsive construction

Soraya Mokhtari

Ph.D. Student

Soraya Mokhtari is a Ph.D. student in the ENSURE Lab at Texas A&M University, with an interdisciplinary background in architecture, urban design, and informatics. Her research focuses on embodied carbon, life cycle assessment (LCA), and the long-term environmental performance of buildings and building materials, with particular interest in the effects of future climate conditions and the application of machine learning methods to building-performance analysis.

Her doctoral research examines how large hygrothermal swings, including changes in temperature and moisture exposure, can influence the deterioration and long-term performance of building-envelope materials and assemblies. By integrating current weather conditions with future climate projections, her work seeks to better understand how increasingly variable and extreme hygrothermal environments may affect envelope performance, maintenance and replacement needs, and long-term environmental impacts. The broader goal of her research is to support more durable, climate-responsive, and lower-carbon building-envelope design and life cycle decision-making.

Research focusEmbodied carbon · LCA · Hygrothermal deterioration · Future climate · Machine learning

Lab alumni · ENSURE Lab

Hassan Anifowose

Ph.D. 2022

Hassan’s doctoral research developed and evaluated a gamified Virtual Reality interface for training construction science students in construction processes. His work assessed the impact of the gamified Virtual Construction Lab on players’ confidence while completing real-world building assembly tasks across levels of progression. A user study revealed a preference order of Player Experience, Level of Progression, Player Behavior and Object Interaction — work that matters for replacing resource-intensive field lab tasks with virtual training.

Varusha Venkatraj

Ph.D. 2022

Varusha completed her Master’s in 2019 on the trade-offs between embodied and operational energy, examining how orientation, envelope thermal properties, glazing type and window-to-wall ratio affect embodied energy. She earned her doctorate in 2022 by pioneering a machine learning algorithm to predict embodied and operational impacts of commercial buildings, generating simulated energy use data to train and test the model. It eased the limits of data availability and computation time, and proved accurate on a commercial building case study in climate zone 2A.

Lena Baker M. Abu-Ennab

Ph.D. 2022 · Civil Eng.

Lena investigated the environmental impacts of incorporating kaolin clay into kaolinite-based calcium sulfoaluminate cement concrete for large-scale 3D printing. Her cradle-to-gate life cycle assessment of mixes at 0%, 5% and 10% kaolin by weight found that 3D-printed solid walls reduce embodied energy, global warming potential and eutrophication potential by roughly 12%, 55% and 4% per unit volume — and by roughly 55%, 77% and 53% per unit of compressive strength.

Jason Bullen

M.S. 2020 · Best Thesis

Jason’s thesis investigated how site logistics factors influence the embodied impacts of construction projects, analysing how site logistics and project planning affect the movement of materials and equipment and potential damage to staged materials and work-in-progress. Using the Delphi method with a panel of construction specialists, he concluded that material and site management substantially influence initial embodied environmental impact. Lab alumni · SPACE Lab (Spatial Processing in Altered and Challenging Environments)

Hannah Park

Ph.D. 2022

Hannah’s work was fundamental to establishing the SPACE Lab — she was the first researcher to define its procedures and protocols for human spatial cognition research. She focused on spatial navigation and spatial representation, examining whether particular learning perspectives suit people with differing cognitive styles, and how field dependence and independence alter navigation strategy and performance. Her research informs spatial navigation during natural disasters, where GPS signals and landmarks may be unavailable.
